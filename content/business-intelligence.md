## 1. Start with a real business problem

Imagine a retail company with **three stores in Bengaluru**. Customers make purchases every day. Thousands of sales transactions are saved, but management still has questions:

- Which store generates the highest sales?
- Which store is performing poorly?
- Are sales improving or declining?
- Where should management focus its attention?

**Storing data is not the same as understanding the business.** The company must analyse the data before making decisions.

## 2. What is Business Intelligence?

**Business Intelligence (BI)** is the process of collecting, preparing, analysing and presenting business data to help people understand performance and make informed decisions.

<div class="note"><strong>Simple definition to remember</strong><div>Business Intelligence helps companies convert raw data into useful insights that support business decisions.</div></div>

<div aria-label="Business Intelligence process" class="bi-process"><span>Raw Data</span><b>→</b><span>Analysis</span><b>→</b><span>Insight</span><b>→</b><span>Decision</span></div>

Charts and dashboards help explain results, but the goal of BI is **better understanding and better decisions**, not just visual design.

## 3. Retail: Which store needs attention?

A retail company compares weekly sales across three stores. The figures below are illustrative.

<div class="notes-table-wrap"><table class="notes-table number-table"><thead><tr><th>Store</th><th>Week 1</th><th>Week 2</th><th>Change</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Store A</td><td>₹1,00,000</td><td>₹1,10,000</td><td class="positive">+10%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Store B</td><td>₹80,000</td><td>₹56,000</td><td class="negative">−30%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Store C</td><td>₹90,000</td><td>₹95,000</td><td class="positive">+5.6%</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="note"><strong>How to calculate percentage change</strong><div><strong>Percentage Change = ((Week 2 − Week 1) ÷ Week 1) × 100</strong><br/>For Store B: ((56,000 − 80,000) ÷ 80,000) × 100 = <strong>−30%</strong>.</div></div>

<div class="insight-panel"><strong>Business insight</strong><p>Store B's sales declined by 30%. Management should investigate possible reasons such as customer traffic, stock availability or competitor activity. The numbers flag the problem, but <strong>do not establish the cause</strong>.</p></div>

## 4. Finance: Where is the budget overspending?

A Technology department has a monthly budget. The Finance Manager wants to compare planned spending with actual spending and find the largest variance driver.

<div class="notes-table-wrap"><table class="notes-table number-table"><thead><tr><th>Expense Category</th><th>Budget</th><th>Actual</th><th>Variance</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Cloud Hosting</td><td>₹4,00,000</td><td>₹5,00,000</td><td class="negative">+₹1,00,000</td></tr>
<tr><td>Software Licences</td><td>₹3,00,000</td><td>₹3,30,000</td><td class="negative">+₹30,000</td></tr>
<tr><td>IT Support</td><td>₹2,00,000</td><td>₹1,80,000</td><td class="positive">−₹20,000</td></tr>
<tr class="total-row"><td>Total</td><td>₹9,00,000</td><td>₹10,10,000</td><td class="negative">+₹1,10,000</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="note"><strong>Budget variance formula</strong><div><strong>Variance = Actual − Budget</strong>. Overall overspending: ₹10,10,000 − ₹9,00,000 = <strong>₹1,10,000</strong>, or about <strong>12.2% of budget</strong>.</div></div>

<div class="insight-panel"><strong>Business insight</strong><p>Cloud Hosting has the largest individual overspend (₹1,00,000). Management can investigate cloud usage, vendor invoices and service costs before deciding on a corrective action.</p></div>

## 5. HR: Which team has the highest attrition?

Employees have resigned from several departments. HR wants to compare resignation rates fairly, even though department sizes differ.

<div class="notes-table-wrap"><table class="notes-table number-table"><thead><tr><th>Department</th><th>Average Employees</th><th>Employees Left</th><th>Attrition Rate</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Sales</td><td>50</td><td>8</td><td class="negative">16%</td></tr>
<tr><td>IT</td><td>80</td><td>4</td><td>5%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Customer Support</td><td>40</td><td>6</td><td class="negative">15%</td></tr>
<tr class="total-row"><td>Total</td><td>170</td><td>18</td><td>10.6%</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>

<div class="note"><strong>Attrition rate formula</strong><div><strong>Attrition Rate = (Employees Left ÷ Average Employees) × 100</strong><br/>Sales: (8 ÷ 50) × 100 = <strong>16%</strong>.</div></div>

<div class="insight-panel"><strong>Business insight</strong><p>Sales has the highest attrition rate (16%). HR should review employee feedback and exit-interview data. This highlights <em>where</em> to investigate, not <em>why</em> people left.</p></div>
